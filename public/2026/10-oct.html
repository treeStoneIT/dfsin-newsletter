Be Fully Present

“Most conversations are just alternating monologue. The question is, is there any real listening going on?” – Leo Buscaglia

Today's message is an excerpt from the book, Positive Connectivity: Essentials to Energizing Sales and Boosting Client Relations by Garry Kinder and Karol Ladd.

Most of us live with a constant flow of distractions that keep us from focusing on the present. For some, being connected to technology becomes a crutch, offering a feeling of importance or productivity. Ironically, as much as we love the devices that keep us connected, they can often be the biggest culprit in keeping us disengaged from the person in front of us. A study by the University of Essex revealed that simply having a cell phone visibly present in the room made people less likely to develop empathy or intimacy during meaningful conversations. Even when no one checked their phone - just having it visibly in the room lowered the level of engagement! Isn’t it amazing to think of the power that little device has in our lives and especially over our ability to listen and focus with others?

It takes a deliberate effort to reduce distractions and strengthen our attention. One of the greatest qualities you can demonstrate in sales or in life is the gift of making others feel sincerely important to you. Your ability to be fully present, making your client feel as though they are the most important person in the room, can have a lasting positive impact. When meeting with a client, consider the following ways to eliminate interruptions.

  • Choose your surroundings wisely.
  • Diminish mental distractions.
  • Be an engaged listener.
  • Write it down.
  • Stay on target with an agenda.

Ted Talk

Hello Everyone,

A recent article in LIMRA identified 6 Forces Reshaping Financial Services Distribution (Article April 2026, Author Cindy Hoes, MBA, Corporate Vice President, Head of Distribution Research, LIMRA and LOMA) as follows:

  • Strategic consolidation and platform building
  • Firm and advisor recruitment, retention and succession planning
  • Evolving consumer preferences and experience expectations
  • Intermediary evolution
  • Demand for holistic planning and wealth management capabilities
  • Technology-driven transformation

These are already taking place at a rapid pace. If we pick a couple of the above, it should be noted in the recruitment, retention and succession category:

McKinsey (research firm) tackles this second force in detail in an online 2025 article, “The looming advisor shortage in US wealth management.” With elevated numbers of financial professionals retiring over the next decade, the industry faces heightened pressure for succession planning, identifying new talent pipelines, and mentoring next-generation talent.

This is already obvious to leadership here in Ontario Central, the ‘talent’ pipeline doesn’t have a strong pool from previous ‘training and development firms’. It seems that those organizations have gone the way of the dodo bird. Finding talent to transition business to the next gen has become harder. Why does that matter… Legacy, your legacy. It’s one thing to sell your business to the next person that comes by with a cheque… but are they competent, will your clients like them, are they in sync with your vision for the business in the future?

And the other one I want to hone in on:

As the convergence of wealth management and protection solutions accelerates, financial professionals are forced to use a fragmented set of tools and platforms to support clients and connect partners within the ecosystem. A LIMRA-EY study, Reimagining Growth, reveals significant upside for carriers and providers that enable integrated experiences, tools and workflows.

Amid intense competition and the great wealth transfer, client acquisition and retention remain a top advisor priority.

If we look at how the regulatory bodies ‘see us’ inclusive of compensation disclosure, this indicates that in order for a client to ‘gain value’ for the compensation, they must receive holistic advice from their advisor, rather than piecemeal transactions.
