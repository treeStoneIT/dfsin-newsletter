Express Appreciation

“Feeling gratitude and not expressing it is like wrapping a present and not giving it.” – William Arthur Ward

Everyone wants to feel valued, yet genuine appreciation is often overlooked. A sincere “thank you” can strengthen relationships, build trust, and create lasting connections.

Yet, in today’s fast-paced world, sincere appreciation is often overlooked. We become so focused on the next appointment, the next sale, or the next goal that we forget the lasting impact of a simple, heartfelt “thank you.”

A powerful example is Edward Spencer, who saved 17 people from the sinking Lady Elgin in 1860, risking his own health in the process. Decades later, when asked what he remembered most, he replied:

“Not one of the 17 people whose lives I saved ever came back to say thank you.”

His words remind us that appreciation matters more than we often realize.

Every day, clients, colleagues, and prospects contribute to our success. Taking a moment to acknowledge their efforts tells them, “I see you. I value you.”

Make gratitude a daily habit. Be sincere, specific, and timely in expressing thanks.

Never underestimate the power of two simple words:

Thank you.

They take only a moment to say but can make a lasting difference.

Shirley’s Spotlight

Who’s in the room when the money moves?

Hello Everyone,

There is a number every advisor should have taped to their monitor: 66%. That is the share of Canadian children who change advisors shortly after receiving an inheritance, according to an Accenture study done with IIROC (1). For widows, it’s 70% within a year of a spouse’s death. The great wealth transfer is usually described as an opportunity. For most books, it’s closer to a test.

Leger released a study in August, Financial Compass: The Great $1 Trillion Wealth Transfer, based on more than 5,000 Canadians (2). Roughly $120 to $150 billion will change hands in the next two years. Only 27% of Canadians who plan to give say they would seek financial advice when a windfall arrives. One in five would consult no one. Nearly half don’t have an advisor at all. Leger’s conclusion was blunt: advisors aren’t being rejected, they just aren’t in the consideration set.

And the most exposed clients aren’t the high-net-worth households everyone is chasing. Leger found they’re mass-affluent, under $500,000 in investable assets, and 68% of them are already thinking about leaving. That describes a large share of the clients we serve.

Here is the uncomfortable part. We don’t lose those assets on the day a client passes. We lose them years earlier, on the day we decided the client’s children weren’t our client. Only 19% of advisors put a high priority on prospecting the 18-to-35 group, which happens to be the largest segment in the country (3).

The encouraging part is what Canadians say they want. The Money Wise Institute found that 67% of Millennials and Gen Z would rather have the inheritance conversation now, and the same share would switch from online tools to a human advisor for more personal service (4). IPC Private Wealth’s survey of affluent Canadians found 40% actually want their heirs to keep the same advisor (5). The door is open. Most of us simply haven’t walked through it.

A challenge for November: pick your ten largest clients over 65. For each one, can you name their children, and have you met them? If the answer is no, that’s the meeting to book before year end. Offer to help the client walk their family through the plan. You’ll be solving a problem for the parent, and you’ll be in the room when the money moves.

Shirley Huang, CIM, FCSI, RIS, CIWM
